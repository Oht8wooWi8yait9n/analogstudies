MMEA Mission Definition & Operations

Mission Definition

The recently established MMEA project plans for missions of approximately 1-year duration starting no earlier than August 2027. A mission is defined as one integrated protocol which may include one or more transit phases and a surface exploration phase. Mission phases and durations are variable and determined by the science and operational requirements for each mission.

Each phase is typically performed in a different analog platform: transit in the Transit Vehicle, and surface exploration in the Surface Habitat and Rover Vehicle, with transfer of crew and supplies via a tunnel linking the platforms.

Reference Mission Conditions

  • Simulated space mission using the MMEA to emulate a non-specific spacecraft and surface habitat
  • 4 to 6 crew members
  • Duration: Up to approximately 1-year confined habitation specific to science requirements; approximately 14 total study months inclusive of 6 weeks pre- and 2 weeks post-confinement training and baseline data collection
  • Light/Dark Cycle (typical): Lights on 0700, lights out 2300, 7 days per week
  • Typical crew day schedule requires 16 hours awake; 8 hours of sleep scheduled
  • Crew mission schedule modeled after ISS crew schedule, modified to reflect exploration mission activities and events
  • Mission scenario constructed to simulate varying degrees of workload and includes specific stressful conditions commonly encountered in space flight
  • Crew remains isolated for the duration of the mission
  • No access to internet content, social media, television/radio, and telephone. However, the crew will receive regular uplinks of selected newspapers
  • Crew members have regularly scheduled private family conference, private medical conference, and private psychological conference
  • Crew members have access to a limited selection of audio and video media, books and training materials through a controlled account
  • Continuous monitoring of crew members during isolation, excluding sleep quarters, hygiene areas, and during private family/medical/psychological conferences

Mission Fidelity

  • Mission Control Center (MCC) for mission appropriate interaction with MMEA crew members:
    • 24/7 mission video surveillance with audio, recorded during mission with long term storage and transfer available on specific request consistent with privacy and confidentiality requirements
    • Voice communication recordings and text communication between crew and MCC during the mission with long term storage and available to payload developers on specific request consistent with privacy and confidentiality requirements
    • Logging of operationally relevant mission events, including off-nominal situations and anomalies
  • Exploration realistic comm delays from seconds to minutes each way for voice and/or text:
    • Simulation of Acquisition of Signal/Loss of Signal (AOS/LOS) of varying duration
    • Simulation of solar conjunction (total comm blackout up to two weeks)
  • Altered MCC and crew schedules to simulate Mars Sol
  • Communication bandwidth realism with priority queuing of data types and file transfers between ground and habitats
  • Flight simulators to support exploration mission scenarios such as docking, rover activities or piloting activities
  • Rover module capable of supporting two crew members for 2–3 days isolated from the Surface Habitat and other crew members
  • The rover module does not physically move but allows for Virtual Reality excursions (driving and EVA):
    • Includes communication with MDA and MCC
  • Virtual reality system for simulated EVA tasks including VR EVAs on treadmills
  • Visually isolated EVA sandbox with equipment for physical EVAs including EVA suits (low fidelity), airlock procedures, In Situ Resource Units, and tools
  • Heart rate monitor to support exercise safety and data collection activities
  • Support for actigraphy and other wearables, including implementation in multiple platforms
  • Support for basic laboratory tools
  • Full facility coverage of surveillance video and audio except for bedrooms, bathrooms and ability to turn off areas for private activities
  • Blood/saliva/urine/fecal collection pre, during and post mission. Crewmembers may be trained for sample processing.
  • Medical workstation:
    • Remote medical procedures and examinations
  • Adjustable LED lighting
  • Stowage modules to house full mission supplies with pass through for hardware, biological samples, and trash
  • Modifiable virtual window views
  • Ability to control habitat temperature for crew comfort
  • Limited exercise equipment (aerobic and resistive) to simulate daily operational activities
  • Simulated habitat and vehicle systems (e.g. Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS), power, and propulsion systems) with physical or electronic user interfaces to support complex operational activities
  • 3D printer to support vehicle maintenance and operational tasks
  • Simulated alarm system to support emergency scenarios
  • Flight-similar galley capabilities for preparing meals. Limited galley in the Transit Vehicle, no galley in Rover, and more equipped galley in the Surface Habitat
  • Plumbed water for crew consumption and food rehydration
  • Shower/sink with hot and cold running water for crew hygiene
  • Ability to monitor and restrict resources such as water, power, consumables, etc.
  • Hydroponics as supplemental food source
  • System spares, consumables and toolkits to facilitate realistic maintenance and repair tasks

Dietary Conditions

Dietary conditions are subject to potential modifications based on user needs. As a proposed baseline participating crew members will be provided a minimum of 3 meals each day. Diet and core menu is based on NASA spaceflight nutritional requirements. Once a crew is selected, specific crew caloric requirements are determined via measurements of resting metabolic rate (RMR). A core menu of primarily off-the-shelf food items is provided with a flight like combination of thermo-stabilized, rehydratable, and natural food items. Due to the objective of stocking all foods required for a complete mission in each habitat, fresh foods are not available in the primary food supply. A hydroponics system will be available to supplement the food supply with crew grown crops.

Typical Crew Daily and Weekly Work Requirements

The operational plan uses the ISS Program nominal 24-hour workday structure as a guideline. Feasibility of studies and technology demonstrations with specific workday organization requirements will be assessed on a per study basis.

Below is the proposed baseline MMEA mission plan:

  • All time spent in the habitat will be working on tasks related to the mission and associated payloads (studies, tech demos) — individual’s professional/work activities outside of the mission scenario are not allowed
  • Crew awake at 0700 and are off duty at 2300 with nominal one shift operation for all crew
  • Sleep period (8.0 hours)
  • Post-sleep period includes morning meal (1.5 hours)
  • Daily planning conferences, medical conferences, work preparation, and plan familiarization (2.0 hours)
  • Work consists of scheduled payload tasks and MMEA operations tasks, i.e. Transit Vehicle/Rover/Surface Habitat maintenance, flight simulator for a spacecraft and/or terrestrial rendezvous mission, public affairs activities, education outreach, etc. (6.5–8.0 hours)
  • Midday meal (1 hour)
  • Exercise period (1.25 – 2.5 hours, includes time for setup, cardiovascular/resistive exercise, stowage, hygiene [cool down and cleanup])
  • Pre-sleep period includes evening meal (2.0 hours)
  • A nominal 7-day work/rest cycle will consist of 5.5 days available for conducting planned mission tasks and payload activities and 1.5 consecutive off-duty days. Housekeeping and 1.0 hour of scheduled work on the weekends is included in the 5.5 working days

Research and Technology Demonstrations

Studies and technology demonstrations suitable for this analog may include, but are not limited to:

  • Crew hierarchy, team cohesion, teamwork and stress
  • Communication and autonomy with varying communication delay
  • Simulation of autonomous operations
  • Multi-Team systems (MCC and Crew)
  • Testing of autonomous and AI assisted technologies
  • Controlled environmental conditions:
    • Temperature
    • Humidity
    • Lighting
  • Simulated habitat, vehicle, and EVA systems
  • Planetary surface exploration (VR and physical)
  • High tempo operations
  • Exploration medical capabilities, assessments and operations
  • Behavioral health and performance assessments
  • Human factors evaluations and HSIA
  • Human health countermeasures testing and assessments
  • In-mission training paradigms
  • Exercise countermeasures
  • Performance evaluations after short periods of sleep deprivation
  • Effect of lack of privacy, cramped space, simulated emergency response, visual monotony, food monotony, etc.
  • Food systems including crop growth
